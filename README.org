MYCIN, the expert system written at Stanford in the 1970s, as an application of [[https://github.com/LispMachineLab/lispm-desktop][LispM Desktop]],
which its App Store installs.  In code, the prefix is ~lispm-mycin-~.

* What it does

MYCIN asks a doctor about a patient and the cultures taken from them, and names the organisms
likely to infect the blood, each with a certainty factor:

#+begin_example
------ ORGANISM-1 ------
Enter the identity (genus) of ORGANISM-1: unknown
The gram stain of ORGANISM-1: neg
Is ORGANISM-1 a rod or coccus (etc.): rod
What is the AEROBICITY of ORGANISM-1? aerobic
Is Sylvia Fischer a compromised host? yes
Is Sylvia Fischer a burn patient?  If so, mild or serious? serious
Findings for ORGANISM-1:
 IDENTITY: ENTEROBACTERIACEAE (0.800)   PSEUDOMONAS (0.760)
#+end_example

Besides an answer it takes =help=, =?= for the answers it accepts, =why= for the rule it is
trying, =rule= for that rule as written, =unknown=, and several answers with certainty factors,
as =(neg .8 pos .2)=.  The window's buttons and the MYCIN menu say the same.

* How it works

This is the MYCIN Peter Norvig rebuilt in /Paradigms of Artificial Intelligence Programming/
(chapter 16): the EMYCIN shell and a sample of rules, in Common Lisp, unchanged in [[file:paip/][paip/]] under
their MIT License.  [[https://github.com/LispMachineLab/emacs-cl][Emacs Common Lisp]] runs them, in a batch Emacs beside yours; the App Store
installs it first.  ~M-x lispm-mycin~ opens a consultation, ~C-c C-n~ starts another.
